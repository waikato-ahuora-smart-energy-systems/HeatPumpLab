Method notes

RHP-A results come from main_RHPdesignA_caps.py: a fixed grid of RHX1 lift (0–125 K, within the 2/3-rule limit) and stage-2 power share (0.40–0.55), with the gas-cooler outlet offset optimised for each design. Its gas-cooler pressure follows from the design, so the limit simply removes designs above it.

SLTHP results come from main_SLTHP_compare.py with no pressure cap: RHX1 lift 30–140 K at a 0.50 power share, with the gas-cooler pressure and RHX1 flow share optimised inside each design. Each design keeps its pressure scan (36 points, 5–60 bar). Under a limit, the page uses the optimum where it fits and otherwise the best scan point below the limit, so the SLTHP under a limit is resolved to about 7 % in pressure.

Design choice: "Highest COP" takes the strict optimum. "Lowest pressure within 1 %" takes the lowest-pressure design whose COP is within 1 % of that optimum. The difference matters at a 30 K approach, where RHP-A at 150–170 °C has a second family of designs with no RHX1 lift at 45–68 bar that ties the recuperated designs at about 20 bar to within 0.04–1 % COP; the strict optimum then jumps between the two. The SLTHP has the same effect once (20/80, 170 °C, 20 K, 43 bar).

Headline pressures: maximum pressure and overall pressure ratio are the highest, and low-stage suction pressure the lowest, across the six air temperatures. For the SLTHP, designs taken from a pressure-scan point (the 1 % rule) use the suction pressure of that lift's optimum, marked ≈.

Live design: the same two cycle models (RecuperativeHeatPump Design A and SplitHeatPump.evaluate) in JavaScript over CoolProp 8 (HEOS mixtures), with the same compressor model (Pareto front isentropic efficiency, motor 0.93). Two things differ from the study. The source water has a finite flow from its inlet to its outlet temperature, and the evaporator pressure is the highest at which the evaporator and superheater stay 5 K below it; the study used an isothermal 40 °C source with the evaporator inlet held at 10 °C. And the RHP-A gas-cooler outlet offset is optimised continuously for COP (the study tried 0, 7, 10, 15, 20 and 30 K in turn until stage 1 was dry). Run on the study's own evaporator setting, the models reproduce the REFPROP COP to within about 0.02 % for the propane, butane and pentane blends, and to about 0.4 % for propane/cyclopentane (CoolProp's mixing parameters). The SLTHP's gas-cooler pressure and RHX1 flow share are inputs; an approach shortfall under 0.3 K is reported rather than failed.

Duties and power: refrigerant mass flow = stage-1 suction density × volumetric efficiency × low-stage swept volume. "Model" volumetric efficiency uses the pressure-ratio correlation in the cycle model (1 − 0.0824·(PR−1)^0.728); "Fixed" applies the chosen value to both cycles. For reference, a commercial screw compressor of this size is listed at 197 m³/h (50 Hz), and a commercial low-stage compressor at 145 m³/h (2950 rpm). High-stage swept volume needed = mass flow / (stage-2 suction density × stage-2 volumetric efficiency). Electrical input includes a motor efficiency of 0.93.
